# 0062 Notes can be coloured by key (`by-key`), with octave palettes

Status: accepted (2026-11-27)

Context: Notes are coloured by pitch (`by-pitch`, ADRs 0017, 0021, 0056): `o2` and `o4` are the same colour, so a score's colours say which pitch sounds but not in which octave. A writer reading a whole score wants to see register as well: which notes are low and which are high, on every instrument at once, the jegogan's told apart from the kantilan's at a glance.

Decision: A second colour scheme, `by-key`, colours each pitched note by its key, the pitch in an octave. The same pitch keeps its hue in every octave and gets lighter as the octave rises: brightness carries the octave, hue the pitch.
- `:scheme by-key` is chosen on a score or a group, as schemes are (ADR 0032). `by-pitch` is unchanged and stays the default, with `pastel1`.
- Octaves are absolute, numbered as keys are: the digit of the key (`o2` is in octave 2), octave 1 being the jegogan's lowest, each octave starting at ding. One number holds across every instrument, so `o2` is the same colour on every instrument that has it.
- `by-key` takes an octave palette: one colour ramp per scale position, a colour per octave from low to high. A scale with more pitches than the palette has ramps reuses them from the first, as palettes do now. The default is `pastel1-octaves`: `pastel1`'s hues in four octaves (1–4, the gong kebyar's register from the jegogan to the kantilan), darkest at octave 1. An octave palette can also be written as one list per scale position, lowest octave first. A plain palette (`pastel1`, or a list of colours) with `by-key` is an error naming `by-key`, rather than quietly colouring by pitch, and an octave palette with `by-pitch` is an error too: each scheme takes its own kind of palette. Every ramp of a palette written as lists has the same number of colours, so the palette has one number of octaves and the nearest-octave rule is unambiguous.
- A key outside the palette's octaves takes the nearest octave's colour (the kantilan's `i5` is drawn in `i4`'s). There is no warning, since the reader has none.
- Under `by-key`, `:colors` takes pitches and keys: a pitch sets it in every octave, a key (`i2` or `ding2`) that key only, and a key wins over its pitch. Under `by-pitch`, `:colors` takes pitches only, as now.
- Strokes stay grey, and the text colour is still white or near-black by contrast with the fill. The figure JSON and the template don't change: each note already carries its fill and text colour.

Consequences: Existing scores and their golden files don't change. Notation (01), the reader (03) and "Note style" (04) gain `by-key` and octave palettes; `(notasi style)`'s palettes take ramps; the glossary gains `by-key` and octave palette. The manual's page on colours, and the Emacs mode's completion, gain the new names. The colours of `pastel1-octaves` are checked by eye on a real score before they are fixed in 04. A writer's own colour procedure named from a score, and a sheet of a palette's colours, come later (backlog).
